# Messages (SMS relay)

Messages relays incoming SMS from the paired Android phone to the Mac and lets you read the phone's conversations and reply from the Mac. Traffic uses the existing authenticated LAN or Bluetooth session; nothing reaches a cloud service, and the Mac keeps SMS content in memory only. Android remains the source of truth: the Mac reads provider lists on demand and never polls or writes SMS storage.

Open Features → Messages on either platform. On Android the panel requests SMS permissions, lists conversations, shows a conversation with its composer directly above the newest message and reports unread counts, skipped rows and live relay counts (relayed, waiting, not relayed); an incoming message updates its conversation in that list at once, so an open panel never waits for a reload to show it. On the Mac the panel selects a connected phone, lists conversations, opens a conversation with its composer directly above the newest message and its history below it, and reports status, the live link and privacy-safe relay counts inline. Mac notifications carry the address as their title, the message text as their body when preview is enabled (a generic “New message” otherwise), an inline Reply action and the default tap that opens Messages.

## Permissions

Android needs three permissions with distinct roles: RECEIVE_SMS for live relay, READ_SMS for conversations and history (and for provider message IDs after a reply), and SEND_SMS for replies. Features → Messages → Allow SMS access requests exactly the missing ones and explains each. If Android permanently denied a permission, or the app was sideloaded on Android 13+, the panel links to App info and explains **Allow restricted settings**. Shared results name the missing permission (result 1) separately from a provider or store failure (result 5), and the Mac shows the same distinction.

With only RECEIVE_SMS, live messages still arrive but carry `live:` message IDs and `addr:` conversation identities, no unread counts and no provider history; granting READ_SMS adds provider threads, message IDs and paging. SMS permissions are Play-restricted and this build is GPL/sideloaded, so store distribution is not claimed.

## Delivery

A send requires an already-secured SMS-capable peer. LAN is preferred; an already-secured Bluetooth channel is used when LAN is unavailable, and a live event retries once on the other live channel after a failure. Mid-page channel loss aborts that page without a completion record, the Mac shows the interruption and asks again from its last completed cursor, and stale request and stream IDs are discarded.

Messages are 1..24,576 UTF-8 bytes in up to eight fragments of 3,072 bytes, verified against a SHA-256 digest before display; NUL is replaced by U+FFFD and never transmitted. One thread list, one history page and one reply may be in flight per peer at a time, each with a 30-second deadline that becomes 60 seconds on a Bluetooth link. A Bluetooth link also gets smaller payloads: the Mac advertises 20 threads and 10 rows there against 50 threads and 50 rows on the local network, and the phone serves within the limits the Mac advertised on that link.

Nothing queues: a reply with no live channel fast-fails with a banner alert instead of waiting, and live events that arrive with no channel are kept in memory for at most 10 minutes (16 events) and flushed on the next channel, reported as **Waiting for the Mac** while parked so the panel never claims a delivery the Mac has not acknowledged. A parked event is offered again every few seconds inside that window rather than waiting for a new channel generation, and the phone re-advertises its capability to a link that has not announced one while the Mac answers a capability record with its own, so a lost fragment, acknowledgement or capability hello costs seconds instead of the message. Neither platform replays anything after process death.

Replies are fragmented by the Mac, reassembled and sent by the phone under the same 30-second deadline, using the default SMS subscription only (dual SIM does not yet offer a per-reply choice). Android shows its own system confirmation for third-party SMS sends; enabling **Remember my choice** on that prompt stops it from returning, and cancelling it reports result 5 to the Mac. Result 0 means every part was accepted by the radio, not that the recipient received it, and short-code billing or concatenation limits can surface as result 5. The Mac keeps an in-memory echo of each sent reply and merges it with the provider row after the next refresh when the default SMS app records it.

## On the Mac

Notifications are posted exactly once per newly announced live message and grouped by normalized address. A provider row that the panel fetched first does not consume the notification: only a posted banner suppresses the next one, so a live event that lands after the panel already showed the provider copy still announces the message. Mac notification authorization is requested once when the app starts, so the prompt cannot swallow the first message. A banner needs all of: aniCe allowed in System Settings → Notifications (a refusal is never re-prompted and must be re-enabled manually), no Focus mode suppressing it, the phone admitting the live message in the first place, and — because macOS shows the frontmost app's own notifications only in Notification Center — the aniCe window not being focused at the time. The phone's own SMS app and a paired watch notify independently of this relay and prove nothing about it. Message text stays hidden until **Show message text in notifications** is enabled per phone in Settings → Messages (default off); the banner then shows the text while Reply keeps working either way. Reply text is clamped to 24,576 bytes on codepoint boundaries. A reply that fails to send or is never confirmed posts a follow-up **Message to <address> failed to send** alert and shows the same error inline. SMS notifications are cleared on quit, and startup clears leftovers under the reserved identifier prefix. The panel reports the live link (Local network or Bluetooth) and counts of live messages received, notifications posted and duplicates suppressed, so a missing banner can be traced to the phone or back to the Mac.

Conversations are grouped by normalized address, so a live-only conversation and its later provider thread remain one card; alphanumeric sender IDs (bank and service senders) key on their trimmed uppercase text, so every sender keeps its own card, rows and notification instead of collapsing into one nameless conversation. The list shows at most the thread limit the link advertises — 50 most recent provider threads on the local network, 20 on Bluetooth — with no older-thread paging in v1, and a completed list replaces the provider snapshot it answered (cards a live message created stay until a later list adopts them). Opening a conversation requests the newest page and merges it under the rows already on screen; **Show older messages** continues from the older cursor the refresh left untouched, so a message that the live relay missed still appears on the next open. History pages carry 10 rows by default (a request may ask up to the link's advertised limit, 50 at most), ordered newest first with a byte-wise messageId tie-break. A provider record that matches a live copy (same normalized address, body digest and a ±5 second timestamp tolerance) replaces it without disturbing a notification already posted for it. Viewing history never marks SMS read; unread counts are provider row counts, not the threads view's all-read flag. Nothing is written to disk, and peer data is dropped when the phone's last channel retires, when trust is removed or when the app quits.

## Android specifics

Thread metadata prefers the semi-internal `content://mms-sms/conversations` view with `canonical-addresses` resolution; any provider failure falls back to the newest `content://sms`/`content://mms` row for that thread, and a thread that cannot be resolved is skipped and counted instead of transmitting a placeholder. Unread counts come from one query over unread incoming SMS rows grouped by thread (`read = 0`); the conversations view's `read` column is a flag meaning "every message is read", so it is never subtracted from the message count, and an unavailable unread query falls back to the newest row's flag. MMS rows do not contribute to unread counts. History queries `content://sms` and `content://mms` directly, decodes text/plain MMS parts with a UTF-8 fallback, sanitizes provider dirt (drafts, empty or undecodable addresses, NUL, oversized bodies, date clamping) and converts MMS seconds to milliseconds. No contacts lookup is performed, so addresses are shown as stored.

SQL paging filters and orders by timestamp only; rows sharing a boundary timestamp are over-included in memory (up to a 2,048-row cap per tie group) and the byte-wise messageId tie-break is applied in Kotlin. The thread list and every served history page are limited to the caps the Mac advertised on that link (50 at most), so a Bluetooth link never receives a local-network-sized payload. A page whose tie group exceeds that cap ends with a cursor so the next request continues. A raw batch that is entirely skipped never ends the scan: the store keeps reading older batches (bounded per request) until it holds a page's worth of displayable rows or the source runs out, so a conversation cannot look empty while older messages exist. A page with no older rows is terminal and carries the `(0, "")` cursor, and the Mac offers **Show older messages** only for a non-terminal cursor. An `addr:` conversation has no provider row, so its history request returns an empty terminal page until the thread list reports a provider thread ID and the Mac re-requests with it.

Skipped rows are totaled and shown as **n rows could not be read** on both the list and the conversation; one unreadable legacy row never fails a page. Group MMS (more than one recipient) and attachment-only MMS are reported unsupported rather than guessed, and MMS cannot be sent through public Android APIs.

## Limits

v1 excludes RCS, MMS send, group MMS content, attachments, contacts resolution, a durable Mac history or search, mark-as-read, dual-SIM reply selection and any background Android SMS reader beyond the manifest receiver (a non-default app receives no MMS broadcast, so incoming MMS text appears on the next refresh). Relay requires the Mac app running with a secured session, and queued events do not survive process death.

OEM telephony providers vary: the conversations view can throw or omit columns, timestamp handling can differ, and an app that is not the default SMS app is not guaranteed to have its sent rows inserted into the provider. A provider that rewrites timestamps outside the ±5 second merge window can leave a duplicate until the next history refresh.

## Verification

- Emulator: grant the three SMS permissions, inject inbound SMS with `adb emu sms send <number> "text"`, verify the Mac notification, the conversation and its history, and a reply; confirm the sent row appears in `content://sms/sent`.
- Physical: real SMS receive and reply, permission-denied states including RECEIVE_SMS without READ_SMS, OEM provider reads through both the conversations view and the direct fallback, live→provider merge after the default SMS app inserts the row, no-live-channel queueing, Mac quit and relaunch behavior, dual-SIM send, and group MMS and attachment-only MMS rejection.

The [wire contract](../../protocol/sms/README.md) defines exact bounds, results and ordering; [DESIGN.md](../design/DESIGN.md) records the panel rules.
