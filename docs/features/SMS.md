# Messages (SMS relay)

Messages relays incoming SMS from the paired Android phone to the Mac and lets you read the phone's conversations, search them and reply from the Mac. Traffic uses the existing authenticated LAN or Bluetooth session; nothing reaches a cloud service, and the Mac keeps SMS content in memory only. Android remains the source of truth: the Mac reads provider lists on demand and never polls or writes SMS storage.

Open Features → Messages on either platform. On Android the panel requests SMS permissions, lists conversations, opens one with its composer directly above the newest message, and reports unread counts, skipped rows and relay counts (relayed, waiting, not relayed); a message received or sent from either panel updates its conversation at once, and a phone with a second active subscription adds a **Send from** choice beside the composer. On the Mac the panel lists conversations a page at a time for the selected phone, opens one with its composer directly above the newest message and history below, searches the phone's messages from a field above the list, refreshes a conversation from its header, and reports status, the live link and privacy-safe relay counts inline. Both panels group rows under day headers and show a SIM chip on a row only in a conversation that mixes two known subscriptions. Mac notifications carry the address as title, the text as body when preview is enabled (**New message** otherwise), an inline Reply and the default tap that opens Messages; Settings → Messages collapses each phone's **Notifications**, **Play sound** and text-preview switches behind a label that reports their state, and the same three sit behind a gear in the panel header.

## Permissions

Android needs three permissions with distinct roles: RECEIVE_SMS for live relay, READ_SMS for conversations, history and search (and for provider message IDs after a reply), and SEND_SMS for replies. Features → Messages → Allow SMS access requests exactly the missing ones and explains each. The block exists only while a permission is missing: the first full grant clears it and the panel reads **SMS access granted** until a Mac link takes that line, a revoked permission restores the block naming only that capability, and Settings → Messages keeps one line naming what is still needed or reporting relay as on. A SIM listing needs READ_PHONE_STATE. The Android panel reads the subscription list once per conversation and requests the permission only when that read would offer a choice; relaying, conversations, search and default-subscription replies all work without it, a missing permission answers result 1 rather than a misleading empty list, and a phone that offers no second subscription shows no SIM control at all. If Android permanently denied a permission, or the app was sideloaded on Android 13+, the panel links to App info and explains **Allow restricted settings**. Shared results name the missing permission (result 1) separately from a provider or store failure (result 5), and the Mac shows the same distinction.

With only RECEIVE_SMS, live messages still arrive but carry `live:` message IDs and `addr:` conversation identities, no unread counts and no provider history; granting READ_SMS adds provider threads, message IDs and paging. SMS permissions are Play-restricted and this build is GPL/sideloaded, so store distribution is not claimed.

## Delivery

A send requires an already-secured SMS-capable peer. LAN is preferred; an already-secured Bluetooth channel is used when LAN is unavailable, and a live event retries once on the other live channel after a failure. Mid-page channel loss aborts that page without a completion record, the Mac shows the interruption and asks again from its last completed cursor, and stale request and stream IDs are discarded.

Messages are 1..24,576 UTF-8 bytes in up to eight fragments of 3,072 bytes, verified against a SHA-256 digest before display; NUL is replaced by U+FFFD and never transmitted. One thread list, one history page, one search page and one reply may be in flight per peer at a time, each with a 30-second deadline that becomes 60 seconds on a Bluetooth link. Unsubmitted records are bound to their request and expire or cancel with it; queue admission is distinct from authenticated transport submission. A new search cancels the previous page in flight, and a mid-page channel loss aborts a search without a completion record. A Bluetooth link also gets smaller payloads: the Mac advertises 20 threads and 10 rows there against 50 threads and 50 rows on the local network, and the phone serves within the limits the Mac advertised on that link.

Replies never queue for an unavailable connection: a reply with no live channel fast-fails with a banner alert instead of waiting, and live events that arrive with no channel are kept in memory for at most 10 minutes (16 events) and flushed on the next channel, reported as **Waiting for the Mac** while parked so the panel never claims a delivery the Mac has not acknowledged. A parked event is offered again every few seconds inside that window rather than waiting for a new channel generation, and the phone re-advertises its capability to a link that has not announced one while the Mac answers a capability record with its own, so a lost fragment, acknowledgement or capability hello costs seconds instead of the message. Neither platform replays anything after process death.

Replies are fragmented by the Mac, reassembled and sent by the phone under the same transport-specific deadline, using the phone's default SMS subscription unless an active one was chosen. A non-zero `sendBegin.subscriptionId` must name an active subscription of the phone: an unknown or inactive one fails with result 4 and is never retried on another SIM, the choice is never persisted, and a SIM listing answers with user-visible labels or result 1 when the phone's READ_PHONE_STATE permission is missing. Android shows its own system confirmation for third-party SMS sends; enabling **Remember my choice** on that prompt stops it from returning, and cancelling it reports result 5 to the Mac. Result 0 means every part was accepted by the radio, not that the recipient received it, and short-code billing or concatenation limits can surface as result 5. The Mac paints the reply as a provisional **Sending** row stamped with the submit time, so an answer that arrives while the phone is still confirming sorts above it, then settles the row into the provider identity and time or removes it when the reply failed or went unconfirmed; a sent row that lands late is re-read once after two seconds so it stops reading **Sending**. A message sent from the phone's own messaging apps is never relayed — Android gives apps that are not the default SMS app no outgoing-message broadcast — so it appears in the Mac's open conversation on the next refresh, one tap under that conversation's header.

## On the Mac

Notifications are submitted once per newly announced live message and grouped by normalized address. A provider row that the panel fetched first does not consume the notification: only a successfully submitted notification suppresses the next one, so a live event that lands after the panel already showed the provider copy still announces the message. Mac notification authorization is requested once when the app starts; submission waits for the authorization response. Notification permission and alert settings are read at launch and when the app becomes active; disabled permission, disabled alerts and submission errors appear inline. The foreground notification delegate requests banners, list presentation and sound. Focus and system presentation settings can still suppress an alert after macOS accepts its submission. The phone's own SMS app and a paired watch notify independently of this relay and prove nothing about it. Message text stays hidden until **Show message text in notifications** is enabled per phone in Settings → Messages (default off); the banner then shows the text while Reply keeps working either way. **Notifications** and **Play sound** are also per phone and default on; turning notifications off stops that phone's banners and sounds — including reply failure alerts — while the relay, the panel's unread counts and the live counters keep running, and sound is unavailable while notifications are off. Reply text is clamped to 24,576 bytes on codepoint boundaries. An unsuccessful or unconfirmed reply posts a follow-up alert and keeps the draft. Because the telephony call cannot be recalled, an uncertain result reads **Not confirmed; it may have been sent** and never automatically resends. SMS notifications are cleared on quit, and startup clears leftovers under the reserved identifier prefix. The panel reports the live link (Local network or Bluetooth) and counts of live messages received, notifications submitted to macOS and duplicates suppressed, so a missing banner can be traced to the phone or back to the Mac.

Conversations are grouped by normalized address, so a live-only conversation and its later provider thread remain one card, and a sender who messages both SIMs stays one conversation whose rows keep their own origin; alphanumeric sender IDs (bank and service senders) key on their trimmed uppercase text, so every sender keeps its own card, rows and notification instead of collapsing into one nameless conversation. The list paints each card as it arrives and loads at most the thread limit the link advertises — 50 threads on the local network, 20 on Bluetooth — 15 rows at a time, continuing from the returned `(dateMillis, threadId)` cursor as the list reaches its end until the phone reports a terminal cursor; a completed first page replaces the provider snapshot it answered (cards a live message created stay until a later list adopts them), and an older page only adds. Opening a conversation requests the newest page and merges it under the rows already on screen; **Show older messages** continues from the older cursor the refresh left untouched, so a message that the live relay missed still appears on the next open. History pages carry 10 rows by default (a request may ask up to the link's advertised limit, 50 at most), ordered newest first with a byte-wise messageId tie-break, and a conversation shows a SIM label on each row only when its known subscriptions number two or more (an outgoing row on the default subscription reads **Phone default**). A provider record that matches a live copy (same normalized address, direction, body digest, a ±5 second timestamp tolerance and an equal subscription, or either subscription unknown) replaces one matching live row without disturbing a notification already posted for it; two SIMs that received the same text at the same moment keep two rows. Viewing history never marks SMS read; unread counts are provider row counts, not the threads view's all-read flag. Nothing is written to disk, and peer data is dropped when the phone's last channel retires, when trust is removed or when the app quits.

A reply uses the phone's default SMS subscription until you choose another. The Mac reads the phone's subscription list once when a ready phone opens the panel and shows **Send from** only when that list offers a second active subscription; the picker marks the phone's default and adds the slot to subscriptions that share a display name, the choice lives for the panel session only, and notification inline replies always use the phone default. A permission gap, a listing failure or a 10-second timeout leaves the panel without a picker, so replies simply use the phone default.

Search matches a substring in SMS bodies and text/plain MMS parts on the phone (ASCII case-insensitive; `\`, `%` and `_` are literal), a query shorter than 2 UTF-8 bytes is not sent, and hits arrive as ordinary message records with provider IDs, ordered and paged exactly like history. **Show more results** walks the cursor; at most 4 pages and 200 rows are held in memory per phone, a newer query cancels the page in flight, and leaving results or retiring the last link drops everything. A search never marks messages read, never posts notifications and never touches thread grouping, and no version can jump to a message from a result.

## Android specifics

The receiver uses `Telephony.Sms.Intents.getMessagesFromIntent` for GSM/CDMA decoding and combines the framework-supplied parts in order only when sender and subscription metadata agree. It preserves the platform timestamp, sanitizes NUL and rejects malformed metadata or oversized bodies. Its broadcast completion is bounded to eight seconds, independently of delivery. No cross-broadcast assembly buffer is maintained.

Thread metadata prefers the semi-internal `content://mms-sms/conversations` view with `canonical-addresses` resolution; any provider failure falls back to the newest `content://sms`/`content://mms` row for that thread, and a thread that cannot be resolved is skipped and counted instead of transmitting a placeholder. Unread counts come from one query over unread incoming SMS rows grouped by thread (`read = 0`); the conversations view's `read` column is a flag meaning "every message is read", so it is never subtracted from the message count, and an unavailable unread query falls back to the newest row's flag. MMS rows do not contribute to unread counts. History queries `content://sms` and `content://mms` directly, decodes text/plain MMS parts with a UTF-8 fallback, sanitizes provider dirt (drafts, empty or undecodable addresses, NUL, oversized bodies, date clamping) and converts MMS seconds to milliseconds. No contacts lookup is performed, so addresses are shown as stored.

SMS and MMS scans retain independent positions and merge their candidate rows in contract order. SQL paging filters and orders by timestamp only; rows sharing a boundary timestamp are over-included in memory (up to a 2,048-row cap per tie group) and the byte-wise messageId tie-break is applied in Kotlin. The thread list pages on the same `(dateMillis, threadId)` cursor: the store reads a `limit + 32` provider window at or below the boundary, resolves unread counts and fallback metadata, then applies the exact cursor filter and byte-wise ordering in memory and reports `more` while eligible rows remain or the window filled, ending a terminal page with the `(0, "")` cursor; the direct `content://sms`/`content://mms` scan is a degraded first-page fallback with no cursor of its own. In search, the store escapes the query for a `LIKE … ESCAPE '\'` match against SMS bodies and text/plain MMS part rows; matching MMS ids are then resolved from the mms table newest-first, so a page boundary never depends on part or insertion order, and a match set beyond the 2,048-row scan cap returns an unsupported-page result without advancing the cursor. Search hits carry their provider thread id so a conversation opened later still resolves its thread. The thread list and every served history or search page are limited to the caps the Mac advertised on that link (50 at most), so a Bluetooth link never receives a local-network-sized payload. A timestamp group larger than 2,048 rows returns an unsupported-page result without advancing the completed cursor. A raw batch that is entirely skipped never ends the scan: the store keeps reading older batches (bounded per request) until it holds a page's worth of displayable rows or the source runs out, so a conversation cannot look empty while older messages exist. A page with no older rows is terminal and carries the `(0, "")` cursor, and the Mac offers **Show older messages** (and **Show more results** for search) only for a non-terminal cursor; the thread list continues itself the same way. An `addr:` conversation has no provider row, so its history request returns an empty terminal page until the thread list reports a provider thread ID and the Mac re-requests with it.

Skipped rows are totaled and shown as **n rows could not be read** on both the list and the conversation; one unreadable legacy row never fails a page. Failed provider sources remain distinct from empty sources; a partial source read does not advance the Mac cursor or replace its completed thread snapshot. Group MMS (more than one recipient) and attachment-only MMS are reported unsupported rather than guessed, and MMS cannot be sent through public Android APIs.

## Limits

The SMS contract is at version 3, so both applications update together and neither can relay or reply against a v2 peer. v3 excludes RCS, MMS send, group MMS content, attachments, contacts resolution, a durable Mac history or search index, mark-as-read, jump-to-message from a search result, per-message thread paging from search rows, and any background Android SMS reader beyond the manifest receiver (a non-default app receives no MMS broadcast, so incoming MMS text appears on the next refresh). A search reads the phone directly and keeps nothing: no index, no query history, no saved results. Relay requires the Mac app running with a secured session, and queued events do not survive process death.

OEM telephony providers vary: the conversations view can throw or omit columns, timestamp handling can differ, and an app that is not the default SMS app is not guaranteed to have its sent rows inserted into the provider. A provider that rewrites timestamps outside the ±5 second merge window can leave a duplicate until the next history refresh.

## Verification

- Emulator: grant the three SMS permissions, inject inbound SMS with `adb emu sms send <number> "text"`, verify the Mac notification, the conversation and its history, and a reply; confirm the sent row appears in `content://sms/sent`.
- Receive: a real SMS shows one Mac banner, one conversation and its unread count; a radio-split multipart SMS arrives as one message; a long Mac→Android reply exercises multi-fragment reassembly; a notification inline Reply reaches the phone and merges with the provider row on the next refresh; either panel sending a message moves that conversation to the top with the new snippet at once, and the Mac's conversation **Refresh** picks up a message sent from the phone's own messaging apps.
- Compose: pressing Send on the Mac paints the reply at once marked **Sending**, and the row settles into the provider identity or disappears when the phone rejects it; a reply that arrives while the send is still being confirmed stays above it; **Return**, **Command-Return** and the phone's keyboard send key all send, while **Shift-Return** keeps the newline; the SIM control sits left of the Send button on the Mac.
- Settings: Settings → Messages shows one collapsed row per phone behind its clickable name label — the label reports **Notifications on/off**, sound and text-preview state — and the Messages panel's gear opens the same three switches for the phone in view.
- Paging: the Mac thread list paints its first page immediately, fills older pages as its last card is reached and stops at the phone's terminal cursor; opening a conversation shows the newest page with day headers, and **Show older messages** continues from the older cursor.
- SIM: on a phone with two active subscriptions the **Send from** control appears and lists user-visible labels (the slot disambiguates subscriptions that share a display name); sending on the second SIM records that subscription in the sent row (`sub_id`); a conversation that received on both SIMs labels each row and keeps two copies of identical text separate; an inactive subscription fails with result 4 and is not retried; a single-subscription phone, or a denied READ_PHONE_STATE, shows no SIM control at all and keeps sending on the default.
- Notifications: turning **Notifications** off for a phone stops its banners, sounds and reply-failure alerts while unread counts and live counters keep updating; turning **Play sound** off keeps the banner silent; both reset to on for a fresh pairing.
- Search: a query streams hits in pages, **Show more results** walks the cursor with the same tie rules, a second query cancels the first, results 1/4/5/6 show their distinct hints, and leaving results or retiring the link drops everything.
- Physical: permission-denied states including RECEIVE_SMS without READ_SMS, OEM provider reads through both the conversations view and the direct fallback, live→provider merge after the default SMS app inserts the row, no-live-channel queueing, Mac quit and relaunch behavior, and group MMS and attachment-only MMS rejection.

The [wire contract](../../protocol/sms/README.md) defines exact bounds, results and ordering; [DESIGN.md](../design/DESIGN.md) records the panel rules.
